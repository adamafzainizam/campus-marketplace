# Draft: permission request to GMI

A draft to adapt and send, not to copy blindly. Read the notes at the bottom before sending.

**Why send it at all.** The site is called "GMI Campus Marketplace", restricts sign-in to institutional accounts, and may move to a domain containing "GMI". Any one of those could reasonably be read as the institute running it. Asking first converts that from a risk into either permission or an early, cheap correction — and it is a far better position than replying to a complaint after the fact.

**Who to send it to.** Whoever owns student affairs, student development, or corporate communications. If you're not sure, your programme coordinator or head of department is a reasonable first stop and can forward it. Send from your GMI student address, not a personal one.

---

## Subject

Permission request: student portfolio project using the GMI name

## Body

Dear [name / Sir or Madam],

My name is [full name], and I am a [year] student in the Diploma in Software Engineering programme at GMI ([student ID]).

As part of building a portfolio for job applications, I have developed a web application called **GMI Campus Marketplace** — a noticeboard where GMI students and staff can buy, sell, or rent secondhand items to each other, such as textbooks, calculators, and small appliances. It is a personal learning project, built and hosted entirely at my own cost, and it does not generate any income.

It is currently live at:
https://campus-marketplace-adamafzainizam.vercel.app

I am writing for two reasons.

**First, to be transparent about it.** Sign-in is restricted to `@gmi.edu.my` addresses and their subdomains, so only members of the GMI community can post listings or send messages. The site states throughout — in its footer, on the sign-in screen, and in a dedicated disclaimer page — that the project is independent and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by the German-Malaysian Institute, and it publishes a full set of terms, a privacy policy, and an acceptable use policy. The acceptable use policy specifically prohibits trading in examination materials, completed assignments, or assignment-writing services.

**Second, to ask permission to keep using the GMI name.** The name is intended to describe who the service is for rather than to imply any official standing, but I recognise that "GMI" is the Institute's to control. I would like to ask whether the Institute is willing to allow its continued use, including in a possible domain name.

I would be glad to make any change the Institute would prefer, including:

- renaming the project so that it does not use the GMI name;
- adding or changing wording anywhere on the site;
- restricting or removing any feature;
- taking the site offline entirely.

If it would be of interest, I would also be happy to demonstrate the project, or to discuss whether it could be useful to the Institute in some official capacity.

I would be grateful for any guidance. Please let me know if there is a more appropriate person or department for this request, and I will write to them instead.

Thank you for your time.

Yours sincerely,
[Full name]
[Student ID] — Diploma in Software Engineering
[GMI email address]
[Phone number, optional]

## Notes before you send

- **Send it from your GMI address.** It identifies you as a student immediately, which is the whole basis of the request.
- **Fill in every bracket.** A template with `[name]` left in it undoes the impression the letter is trying to make.
- **Don't buy the domain until you have a reply.** That's the point of sending this first.
- **Offering to take it offline is deliberate.** It costs you almost nothing — the code, the write-up, and the interview story all survive a rename — and it is the sentence most likely to get a "yes, that's fine". A request that already concedes the worst case is much easier to grant.
- **Keep the reply.** Whatever they say, save it. If it's a yes, that email is your evidence. If there's no reply after two or three weeks, a polite follow-up is reasonable; sustained silence is not permission, so weigh that before committing money to a name.
- **If the answer is no**, it costs you a rename and nothing else. The project keeps working, the case study arguably improves, and you'll have handled it properly.
